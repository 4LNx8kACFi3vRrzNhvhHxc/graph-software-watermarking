# Section 4.4: recognition of Dijkstra graphs

`sh scripts/section44/run.sh` (from the repository root) reproduces the figures of Section 4.4:

1. all Dijkstra graphs with at most 8 vertices, generated by labeled derivations and enumerated up to
   isomorphisms that preserve the entry, are given to `dijkstra_check` (repository) and to
   `dijkstra_check_labeled` (Algorithm 3, `src/dijkstra/dijkstra_labeled.c`);
2. 60,000 random flow graphs (seed 1) are given to both; `rand60k_isdg.txt` says which of them are
   Dijkstra graphs with entry 0; each call to `dijkstra_check` runs in a child process with a 0.1 s limit,
   and a call that does not finish is reported as non-terminating;
3. every graph produced by `watermark_encode` for identifiers of 1 to 14 bits, over every random choice
   of the encoder, is given to both. The harness replaces the two random choices of `watermark_encode`
   (`get_backedge_index` and `encoder_choose_forward_at_2`) by functions that enumerate all their results.

If libfec is not installed system-wide, set `EXTRA_CFLAGS="-I/path/include"` and
`EXTRA_LDFLAGS="-L/path/lib -Wl,-rpath,/path/lib"`.

The procedure of [BENTO:2019] is implemented as published (`dijkstra_check_bento2019`: closedness
conditions (1)-(3), no labels, no condition on the entry), with contractions repeated at each vertex
(`once = 0`, reported in the paper) or at most one contraction per vertex (`once = 1`, as in its pseudo-code).

`dijkstra_check` is an earlier implementation of the recognition procedure kept for reference; it may accept
graphs that are not Dijkstra graphs and may not terminate on graphs without an exit, so the scheme uses
`dijkstra_check_labeled`.
